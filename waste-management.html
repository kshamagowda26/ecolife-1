Circular Waste Hierarchy

Waste Segregation & Management

Waste is merely a misplaced resource. By segregating at the source, over 80% of municipal domestic solid waste can be composted, mechanically recycled, or repurposed into clean energy.

Primary Categories

The Four Waste Streams Explained

Never mix these categories. Cross-contamination destroys recyclable batches and ruins composting.

🍏

Wet Waste (Organics)

Green Bin

Biodegradable items that break down naturally.

Includes:

  • • Fruit & vegetable peels
  • • Leftover food scraps & bones
  • • Coffee grounds & tea leaves
  • • Fallen garden leaves & flowers
📦

Dry Waste (Recyclables)

Blue Bin

Clean, dry, non-biodegradable manufactured items.

Includes:

  • • Clean cardboard & office paper
  • • Rinsed PET plastic bottles & jars
  • • Aluminum soda cans & tin tins
  • • Glass beverage bottles
🔌

E-Waste (Electronics)

Special E-Drop Box

Devices with circuits, batteries, or power cords.

Includes:

  • • Old smartphones & chargers
  • • Laptops, tablets & batteries
  • • Broken LED bulbs & wiring
  • • Computer keyboards & mice
⚠️

Hazardous Waste

Red / Yellow Bin

Substances toxic to sanitation workers and soils.

Includes:

  • • Expired medicines & syringes
  • • Paint cans, solvents & thinners
  • • Pesticides & harsh chemical acids
  • • Motor oil & mercury thermometers

Closed-Loop Flow

The Modern Waste Management Lifecycle

From doorstep collection to municipal recovery facilities.

Step 01

Source Segregation

Separating organics, recyclables, and hazardous items inside homes, commercial kitchens, and schools before municipal pickup.

Step 02

Segregated Transit

Compartmentalized sanitation trucks ensure separated streams never mix during daily city routes.

Step 03

Material Recovery

Wet waste enters industrial anaerobic digesters or windrow composting. Dry recyclables enter automated optical sorting facilities.

Step 04

Resource Rebirth

Rich organic compost enriches municipal parks; clean polymers and metals re-enter manufacturing pipelines without landfilling.

Clear Guidelines

Waste Segregation: Do’s & Don’ts

✓ Do's

  • • Rinse food residue from plastic, glass, and metal containers before putting them in the recycling bin.
  • • Flatten cardboard boxes to save truck space and prevent jams at recycling centers.
  • • Keep batteries taped at the terminal ends to prevent short-circuit sparks in e-waste dropboxes.
  • • Compost fruit & vegetable peels in backyard soil or municipal organic bins.

✕ Don'ts

  • • Never bag recyclables in black trash bags. Sorting machines cannot scan them and they get thrown to landfills.
  • • Don't recycle greasy pizza boxes. Oil soaks into paper fibers and ruins the entire cardboard repulping vat.
  • • Never throw lithium batteries into household bins. They cause dangerous explosive fires in collection compactors.
  • • Don't flush expired medicines down the toilet—they contaminate municipal water reservoirs.
