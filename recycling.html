Circular Material Economy

Understanding the Science of Recycling

Recycling transforms post-consumer waste into valuable raw inputs. Learn what materials can truly be remanufactured, how the closed loop works, and how to avoid the trap of "wishcycling".

The Closed-Loop Principle

What is True Recycling?

Recycling is the industrial process of collecting, sorting, processing, and remanufacturing discarded materials into new products instead of disposing of them in landfills or incinerators.

For example, recycling an aluminum beverage can saves up to 95% of the energy needed to extract virgin aluminum from bauxite ore, and aluminum can be infinitely recycled without degrading in quality.

Recyclability Breakdown

Materials That Can Be Recycled

Filter by category to check preparation rules and recycling efficiency.

📦

Corrugated Cardboard

High Recyclability (5–7 cycles)

Shipping cartons, food boxes (without wax liners), clean packaging pads.

Preparation: Remove heavy tape, flatten boxes completely, keep dry.
🧴

Polyethylene Terephthalate (#1 PET)

Widely Recyclable

Transparent beverage bottles, cooking oil bottles, peanut butter jars.

Preparation: Rinse clean, re-attach cap firmly, never crush flat.
🥛

High-Density Polyethylene (#2 HDPE)

Widely Recyclable

Opaque milk jugs, laundry detergent bottles, shampoo containers.

Preparation: Empty completely, rinse soap film, replace caps.
🥫

Aluminum Beverage Cans

Infinite Recyclability (100%)

Soda cans, sparkling water cans, clean aluminum baking foil (balled up).

Preparation: Quick rinse; can be remelted and back on shelves in 60 days.
🥫

Steel & Tin Food Cans

Infinite Recyclability

Canned soup, pet food tins, crushed tomato cans.

Preparation: Rinse thoroughly, press lid inside can to protect handlers.
🍾

Clear & Amber Glass Jars

Infinite Recyclability

Beverage bottles, pasta sauce jars, pickle containers.

Preparation: Rinse clean; never mix with heat-resistant Pyrex or window glass.
Closed-Loop Engineering

The Step-by-Step Recycling Lifecycle

1

Collection & Curbside Hauling

Recyclable dry waste is collected from residential and commercial receptacles and transported directly to regional Material Recovery Facilities (MRF).

2

Optical & Mechanical Sorting

Rotating trommels separate cardboard; eddy current magnets eject non-ferrous aluminum; near-infrared (NIR) sensors sort plastics by resin code at 30 items per second.

3

Baling, Shredding & Washing

Separated commodities are compacted into 1,000-pound bales. Polymers are shredded into small flakes, washed in hot caustic baths to dissolve adhesives, and sanitized.

4

Pelletizing, Melting & Reprocessing

Clean plastic flakes are melted and extruded into uniform nurdles (pellets); aluminum and steel are cast into ingots; paper pulp is screened and rolled into fresh sheets.

5

New Consumer Product Rebirth

Pellets and ingots return to factories to manufacture new beverage cans, automotive parts, cardboard shipping boxes, fleece outerwear, and sustainable packaging.

Quality Control

Common Recycling Mistakes: Stop "Wishcycling"

Wishcycling occurs when you toss non-recyclable items into the blue bin hoping they will be recycled. In reality, it contaminates whole truckloads.

🛍️

Plastic Grocery Bags in Bins

Film plastics tangle in sorting conveyor axels, forcing entire plants to halt operations for hours. Return bags to grocery store dropboxes instead.

☕

Paper Coffee Cups

Takeaway coffee cups contain a hidden polyethylene waterproof plastic film lining that paper repulping facilities cannot dissolve. Use a reusable travel mug.

🍕

Greasy Pizza Box Bottoms

Oils and food stains do not break down in water vats during paper recycling, destroying the tensile strength of cardboard fibers. Tear off clean lids to recycle; compost the oily base!
